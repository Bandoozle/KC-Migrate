Television advertising is one of the most efficient ways to build a brand in Canada. For the investment, few channels put your message in front of as many people, as memorably, as television does. What has changed is how you buy it and how you measure it. Between traditional broadcast and streaming, you can now reach a mass audience and a narrowly defined one from the same campaign, and track the outcome with a rigour the medium never used to allow. The real question is not whether TV works. It is whether you are buying it the right way.

Why television is such an efficient buy

Television puts your brand in front of a large audience in a single moment, with sight, sound and motion working together. No other channel builds broad awareness as quickly, and when you measure the cost against the number of people reached, few investments compare. A well-made spot tells a story a search ad cannot, and it carries a credibility a banner will never have. People trust brands they see on television, and for a local or regional business working to become a household name, that trust compounds every time the ad runs.

Streaming has changed television advertising

The biggest shift in the last decade is where people watch. Millions of Canadians have moved from cable to streaming, and the major platforms now sell advertising against that audience. Ad-supported tiers on services like Netflix, Amazon Prime Video and Disney+ have opened inventory nobody could buy a few years ago, and they reach viewers, especially younger ones, who no longer watch traditional broadcast at all. If your audience has cut the cord, streaming is how you find them on the same screen in their living room.

Streaming, often called connected TV, also brings the precision of digital to the biggest screen in the house. You can target by geography, household, age and interest, show your ad only to the customers most likely to buy, and pay for completed views rather than guesses. Each campaign reports impressions, completed views and engagement, and it can tie ad exposure to website visits and conversions. You get the reach and impact of television with the aim and accountability of a digital buy.

Broadcast and streaming work best together

Broadcast, or linear TV, is still the buy for mass reach in a market: local news, live sports and prime-time programming put your name in front of an entire city or region at once. Streaming sharpens the aim and captures the audience broadcast no longer reaches. Used together, they cover both the broad base and the specific customer you most want, and a good media plan balances the two based on who you are trying to reach.

You can actually measure it now

The old knock against television was measurement. That objection no longer holds. Streaming reports results directly, and even broadcast can be measured through brand-lift studies, spikes in branded search, traffic to a dedicated landing page, or a promo code tied to the campaign. When a television buy is planned with measurement built in from the start, you learn what it returned rather than guessing.

What it costs, and how to think about the investment

Television carries two costs: producing the commercial and buying the airtime. Production ranges widely depending on how polished the spot needs to be, and the media investment depends on the market, the programming and how often your ad runs. The mistake to avoid is spreading a small budget so thin nobody sees the ad enough times to remember it. Frequency matters. A campaign heavy enough to be noticed in a focused window will outperform the same investment scattered across a year.

When television advertising makes sense for your business

Television earns its place when you want to build a brand rather than chase a single click, when you sell to a broad local or regional audience, and when you can commit enough frequency for the message to land. A home services company, an auto dealership, a healthcare group or a regional retailer are all natural fits. It makes less sense when the budget is too small to reach meaningful frequency. An honest media partner will tell you which situation you are in before taking your investment.

Getting the buy right is the whole game

The difference between television that builds a business and television that burns a budget comes down to the buy: the right stations and platforms, the right frequency and the negotiation behind the rate. This is where experience pays for itself. A strong media buyer knows which placements deliver your audience and how to secure them for less.
